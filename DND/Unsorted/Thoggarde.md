#poi 
_"Every traveler arrives with a story. Every citizen arrives for a second chance."_

Perched on the Southern shores of [[Mithrem]], across the sea from Wafeld and beneath the shadow of the northern mountains, **Thoggarde** is a bustling port city built around freedom, trade, and exploration. The city serves as the final stop for adventurers seeking the legendary Astral Gate, a mysterious portal hidden in the mountains beyond.

Unlike many cities in Alverra, newcomers are welcomed regardless of their origin, status, or past. Exiles, merchants, explorers, scholars, and wanderers all find a home in Thoggarde. The city is known for its lively taverns, diverse population, and culture of acceptance, earning it the nickname:

**"The City of Second Chances."**


# Power in the Overarching World

While Wafeld controls wealth and Aetheris controls magical knowledge, Thoggarde controls something equally valuable:

### Access

Every expedition heading toward:

- [[The Astral Gate]]
- [[Howling Ridge|The Northern Mountains]]
- [[Khaz-Durak]]
- Ancient ruins beyond the frontier

must pass through [[Thoggarde]].

As a result:

- Explorers spend fortunes here.
- Scholars establish research lodges here.
- Mercenary companies recruit here.
- Dwarven traders from Khaz-Durak regularly visit.

Many of the world's greatest discoveries began in a tavern or guildhall in Thoggarde.

Though not politically powerful, the city is incredibly influential because information flows through it constantly.

# Deep Culture

Most Thoggardians believe a person's past matters less than their future.

Questions like:

- "Who were your parents?"
- "Where are you from?"

are considered less important than:

- "What do you plan to become?"

The city attracts people seeking:

- Freedom
- Opportunity
- Redemption
- Discovery

As a result, social status is unusually fluid.

A former criminal might become a respected merchant.

A wandering adventurer might found a guild.

A runaway noble might disappear into ordinary life.

The people care little for titles.

They care about what you contribute.

## The Pilgrim Spirit

Many citizens never intend to remain permanently.

They arrive chasing a dream.

Some leave.

Some fail.

Some decide they have finally found home.

This has created a culture that embraces change rather than fearing it.

# History

Over 800 years ago, an archmage named [[Aldren Thorne]] discovered evidence of a stable passage leading toward the Astral Plane.

Fearing misuse, he established a series of magical trials and safeguards throughout the mountains.

According to official records, these tests were designed to ensure only worthy seekers could approach the Astral Gate.

Most citizens don't believe that story.

The popular belief is far more dramatic:

> The Archmage didn't build the defenses.

> He found them.

Many locals believe the Astral Gate predates civilization itself and is protected by primordial forces older than the gods.

No evidence has ever confirmed either theory.

The debate continues to this day.

# [[The Council of Wayfinders]]
_"No one rules Thoggarde. We merely keep the roads open."_

The Council consists of **nine elected Wayfinders**, each representing a pillar of the city:

- **High Wayfinder** – Presides over the council and acts as the city's chief diplomat.
- **Harbormaster** – Oversees trade, shipping, and foreign relations.
- **Guildspeaker** – Represents merchants, artisans, and local guilds.
- **Warden of the Roads** – Responsible for law enforcement and the safety of travelers.
- **Master Cartographer** – Oversees exploration, maps, and expeditions to the Astral Gate.
- **Keeper of the Moonspire** – High priest or priestess of Sehanine Moonbow, ensuring the city's spiritual well-being.
- **Voice of the People** – Elected by the citizens to represent common interests.
- **Envoy of Khaz-Durak** – A permanent dwarven representative, reflecting the close alliance between the two cities.
- **Lorekeeper** – Curator of the Astral Archive and advisor on magical and historical matters.

The council governs by consensus rather than decree, reinforcing Thoggarde's reputation as a welcoming city where no single individual holds absolute power. The system also creates plenty of opportunities for political intrigue, rival agendas, and quests without making the city feel corrupt or oppressive.
