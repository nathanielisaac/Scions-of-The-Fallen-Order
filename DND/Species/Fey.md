Fey creatures occupy a boundary that does not officially exist in any of the gods' own accounts of creation: the overlap between [[Melora]]'s wild, ungoverned natural world and [[Sehanine Moonbow|Sehanine]]'s domain of dreams and the threshold between waking and sleep. Neither goddess claims to have created fey deliberately. Both privately suspect the other did, and neither has ever pressed the question, since both consider fey creatures more charming than threatening and see no urgent need to resolve their origin.

The most common theological account, favored by the [[Moonbow Covenant]], holds that fey are what happens when a wild place becomes old and isolated enough that the boundary between waking nature and the dream-plane grows thin without ever fully tearing. A forest deep enough, undisturbed long enough, eventually produces something that is not quite animal, not quite spirit, and not quite dream — a fey creature, native to the seam between [[Melora]]'s wildness and [[Sehanine]]'s mystery.

The [[Feywild]]
Where this boundary-thinning becomes extreme and stable rather than incidental, it produces a wholly separate plane: the [[Feywild]], a reflection of Nyr's natural world seen through the dream-plane's logic, where time moves strangely and Melora's wildness is given a vividness and danger that the ordinary material world tempers. The Feywild is not a place either Melora or Sehanine rules outright — it is closer to a shared frontier neither goddess has bothered to formally divide.
