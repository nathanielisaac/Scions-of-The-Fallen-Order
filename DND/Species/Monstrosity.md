Monstrosities are, almost by definition, creatures that do not trace cleanly to a single divine origin. They are what results when natural life — ordinary beasts, ordinary plants, ordinary humanoid stock — is touched, warped, or layered over by a second source of influence it was never meant to carry: residual elemental force, fading divine attention, old [[Panzurite]] corruption, or the lingering aftereffects of a region where two domains' influence overlapped without either god noticing or intervening.

This is why monstrosities are so wildly inconsistent in form and behavior compared to other creature types — there is no single Monstrosity Age, no single monstrosity-making god, only a long, ongoing accumulation of accidents. A monstrosity discovered in a remote mountain range and a monstrosity discovered in a collapsed dungeon may have entirely unrelated origins despite both technically belonging to the same broad category.

The Circus Creatures
The fleshy, tentacled amalgamation and the slimy four-legged creature encountered in the traveling circus's cages are clean examples of this category in the current campaign: not natural beasts, not deliberate constructs, but something assembled or warped by influences the circus's owners likely do not fully understand or control. Their exact origin can remain unspecified — that ambiguity is itself appropriate to the category.
