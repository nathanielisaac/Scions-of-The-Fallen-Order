Fiends, broadly, are beings whose existence depends on sustained corruption — not a single wicked act, but a long enough accumulation of cruelty, malice, or entropy that the accumulation itself takes on independent form and survives the original cause. [[Nyr]]'s fiends do not trace to one single origin point. They are better understood as a category of consequence: what happens when wrongdoing is given enough time, attention, or divine interest to stop being merely behavior and start being substance.

[[Panzuriel]]'s Fingerprints
The most theologically significant fiends in [[Nyr]]'s current age carry [[Panzuriel]]'s particular signature: not violent destruction, but patient, suggestion-driven corruption that convinces its host the choice was always their own. Lesser fiends summoned through careless ritual or unstable planar contact rarely carry this signature so clearly — they are closer to raw malice given temporary shape — but anything resembling sustained, intelligent manipulation in a fiendish encounter should, narratively, feel like it shares [[Panzuriel]]'s fingerprints, whether or not the god himself is involved.

DM NOTE  This document does not assert that fiends from other planes (devils, demons, the Nine Hells as already documented separately) have any direct relationship to Panzuriel. They remain their own self-contained category, exactly as the Nine Hells reference document establishes. The connection drawn here is thematic and tonal — a shared 'flavor' of corruption — not a claim that Panzuriel rules or created the Nine Hells' inhabitants. Keep these separate unless you deliberately choose to merge them later.

Made Fiends vs. Born Fiends
Nyr's oldest theological texts distinguish between fiends that were always fiends — entities whose entire existence began in corrupted form, with no prior innocent state — and fiends that became fiendish, mortal or once-mortal beings whose accumulated choices eventually transformed them. The second category is considered far more tragic by most clergies, and far more useful as a cautionary subject in Gaed's and Bahamut's respective teachings, since it implies the transformation could, in theory, have been stopped earlier.
