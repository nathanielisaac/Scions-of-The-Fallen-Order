Celestials are not a single species or a single god's creation; they are a category of being defined by function rather than origin — extensions of divine will, given independent form so that a god's intention can act somewhere the god's full presence cannot or should not go. Every god capable of producing celestials has done so somewhat differently, and the resulting celestial hierarchies reflect their makers' personalities as much as any shared origin.

Astral Devas and the Servants of Distant Gods
Astral Devas, among the most powerful celestials encountered in the Astral Plane, generally serve no single deity exclusively, instead operating under a loose, ancient mandate to preserve order and protect the innocent within the Astral itself — a mandate old enough that even the Moonbow Covenant cannot trace its origin to one specific divine act. It was an Astral Deva, not affiliated with any deity active in the current campaign's main plot, who rescued Mialee Chitri from the previous Avatar's confrontation near Myrkul's corpse. This is consistent with Astral Deva behavior generally: intervention according to old, internally consistent principles, not allegiance to any particular pantheon member.

Bahamut's Servants
Bahamut's celestial servants — most visibly the small number of ancient gold dragons who accompany him in disguised mortal form, such as the canaries of the old stories — are drawn exclusively from dragons who have demonstrated the same transcendence of pure power toward protection that defined Bahamut's own ascension. They are, in effect, celestials recruited from an already-divine bloodline rather than created from raw material, a detail that makes Bahamut's celestial hierarchy unusually small and unusually personal compared to other gods'.

Sehanine's Quiet Servants
Sehanine Moonbow maintains the smallest, least formal celestial presence of any major deity in Nyr — fitting, given her domain. Where celestials serving her exist at all, they rarely announce themselves as celestials; they appear in the same indirect, threshold-adjacent forms Sehanine herself favors. The white owl that recurs throughout Sehanine's own mythology is, by several Covenant scholars' private theory, not a symbol at all but an actual minor celestial form she has simply never confirmed or denied using personally.
