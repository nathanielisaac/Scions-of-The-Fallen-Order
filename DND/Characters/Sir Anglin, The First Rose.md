#ImportantNPC 
Full name - John Anglin

Long before Wafeld fractured beneath corruption, before noble houses schemed for power and before kings became symbols instead of rulers, there was Sir Anglin.

He was already a knight when the other Roses were squires.

Already a veteran when they earned their spurs.

Already a legend when they became heroes.

Born to a minor household that has long since vanished from history, Anglin rose through merit alone. He possessed no great title, no magical bloodline, and no divine blessing. What he did possess was absolute discipline.

He believed a kingdom survived only because some men were willing to sacrifice everything for it.

Not glory.

Not honor.

Everything.

Over decades he fought in border wars, suppressed rebellions, hunted monsters, and defended villages forgotten by their lords. He watched idealists die. He watched good kings fail. He watched heroes become villains.

And every lesson taught him the same thing:

**Mercy without strength creates chaos.**

**Compassion without sacrifice creates weakness.**

**Order must survive, no matter the cost.**

When the Three Roses were formed, Anglin became their eldest member and unofficial leader. The people loved the younger Roses. They sang songs about them.

Anglin never cared.

He wasn't interested in being loved.

Only effective.

As years passed, his reputation became contradictory.

To peasants he was a protector.

To criminals he was a nightmare.

To fellow knights he was a standard impossible to reach.

And to his enemies he was a man who simply never stopped.

Then came age.

The one foe he could not defeat.

His hair silvered.

His body slowed.

His peers retired or died.

Yet Anglin endured.

Some say he continued fighting because he loved Wafeld.

Others say he no longer knew how to be anything except a knight.

Perhaps both are true.

### Why Anglin Hates Failure

The reason Anglin was so quick to kill Kaelen is because he sees something of himself in him.

Both are men who made impossible choices.

Both carry blood on their hands for what they believed was the greater good.

The difference is that Kaelen questions himself.

Anglin doesn't.

Not anymore.

Anglin buried doubt decades ago.

To him, hesitation is how kingdoms die.

Which is why Sir Anglin remains so dangerous.

Not because he's evil.

Not because he's cruel.

But because he genuinely believes every action he takes is necessary.

And after a lifetime of sacrifice, victory, and loss, he has become too old and too stubborn to believe he could be wrong.

People still repeat an old saying about him:

> "The younger Roses taught Wafeld how to dream.
> 
> Sir Anglin taught it how to survive."
