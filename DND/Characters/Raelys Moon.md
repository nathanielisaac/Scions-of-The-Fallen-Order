#PlayerCharacter 
She grew up in a remote elven settlement deep within the green hush of old-growth forest. The village was quiet, peaceful, and suspicious of outsiders — exactly the kind of place where a child could learn to move unseen, loose arrows with effortless grace, and vanish into the trees before a branch finished swaying. She wasn’t a troublemaker, but she was curious, and curiosity, as the elders said, never travels alone. What set her apart from other young elves wasn’t her skill with a bow — though she had that in abundance — but the strange, unbreakable calm within her. Even as a child, nothing rattled her. Panic never touched her voice, and fear slid off her like dew off leaves. Some called it a blessing from Corellon. Others whispered it was unnatural. She ignored both. What she did not ignore was the silver locket. It was her only inheritance from her mother, who died before she could remember her. The locket was simple, unimpressive to most eyes, but inside it was a pressed midnight-blue flower that never wilted. Her mother had called it a moonbloom — a flower said to grow only once every century under perfect moonlight. To her, it was more than a keepsake. It was her connection to a parent she never knew, and a piece of her identity she didn’t fully understand. And then one night, it was stolen. The thief didn’t break in. They didn’t leave tracks. They didn’t disturb a single thing except the small wooden box where the locket had been kept. The only proof someone had been there at all was a [[Nyrissa|thin red thread snagged]] on the window latch — a thread not from her world. The elders dismissed it. “It’s only a trinket,” they said. “Let it go.” She didn’t. Something inside her hardened. She packed lightly, took her bow, and walked out of the village before dawn. She didn’t make vows, didn’t swear vengeance, didn’t rage. She simply acted — calm, quiet, certain. The world was wide, full of thieves, slavers, smugglers, and collectors of rare things. Someone out there had decided her locket was valuable. And she had only one rule when value was involved: If something is worth stealing from me, it’s worth stealing back. Since then, she has lived as a wanderer, scout, and thief when necessary. She keeps her loyalty close and her freedom closer. She takes jobs that put her near the rich, the powerful, the mysterious — anyone who might possess knowledge of rare magical items or forbidden trade networks. Every whisper of a traveling collector, every rumor of planar traffickers, every mention of a red-threaded cloak sends her tracking silently through the night. She never raises her voice. She never hesitates. And she never lets go of a trail once she’s found it. One day, she will find the locket. And when she does, whoever stole it will learn that calm does not mean gentle
