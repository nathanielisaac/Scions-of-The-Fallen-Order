### THE ORDER OF THE SILVER VOW
##### A knightly brotherhood with chapters on every continent
"Strength is owed to those who cannot demand it"

The Order of the Silver Vow is the closest thing [[Nyr]] has to a unified knightly tradition independent of any single crown — a brotherhood of warriors bound not by birth or national loyalty but by a code descended, however indirectly, from Bahamut's own ideals of protection earned through strength rather than strength claimed through dominance. Where House Ardent represents Aethelgard's broken nobility and its grief over what Kaelen's exile cost the kingdom, the Silver Vow represents something older and less political: an actual living tradition of the kind of knighthood Kaelen himself once embodied, before Oakhaven.

The Vow
Every initiate of the Order swears the same three-part vow at the moment of their knighting, and the Order takes the wording seriously enough that violating any clause, even once, can result in formal expulsion:
	To draw strength only in defense of those who cannot defend themselves.
	To answer for every blow struck, in this life or whatever comes after it.
	To never let silence protect a comrade's wrongdoing, nor let judgment fall on the innocent in a comrade's place.

DM NOTE  That third clause is the one most directly relevant to Kaelen. The Silver Vow's entire code is built around never letting someone else carry a knight's guilt — which is precisely the opposite of what Kaelen chose to do for his King at Oakhaven. If the Order ever learns the full truth of what happened, their judgment of him will not be about the killing. It will be about the silence.

The Order and the Party
The Silver Vow has had no direct contact with the party yet, which makes it one of the cleanest factions in this document to introduce on your own timeline. Its natural entry points are Kaelen (as a living question the Order's code was built to answer) and Ignael (whose paladin training and Asgorath-blessed strength would be immediately recognizable to any Vowed Knight as the kind of power their code exists to govern).
